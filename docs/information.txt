# Sheet 1: AEIOU Summary

## Project

Hostel / PG Management System
Product direction: sellable platform for multiple hostels / PGs (multi-hostel ready)
Tech stack: Angular 19+ (PWA — installable on phone), Node.js backend, MySQL

## A - Activities

New tenant registration and profile creation
Room search, room allocation, and room change
Monthly fee payment and receipt generation
Complaint submission, tracking, and resolution
Admin monitoring of occupancy, dues, and notices

### General observations

Most tasks are repetitive and time-bound
Delays happen due to manual registers and calls
Users prefer mobile-friendly and quick workflows

### Elements / special notes

Fast forms, clear status tags, and instant alerts are critical
Real-time occupancy and fee status reduce confusion
Installable PWA so tenants use it like a phone app without Play Store

## E - Environment

Hostel reception, manager office, and student rooms
Shared physical environment with high daily traffic
Mix of digital and paper-based operations in many hostels

### Environment features

Front desk handles registration and queries
Notice board is common communication point
Payment and complaint handling often happens in office hours

### Scenes

Admission season (high load)
Monthly fee week (peak transaction volume)
Maintenance issue hours (urgent complaint handling)

## I - Interactions

Tenant <-> Admin: registration, fees, complaints
Admin <-> Warden/Staff: maintenance and approvals
Parent/Guardian <-> Admin: fee and safety queries

### How interactions happen

In-person discussion, phone calls, chat, and web/app portal
System-generated status updates reduce repeated follow-ups
Tenant primarily uses installable PWA on mobile; admin uses desktop or tablet

### Special notes

Transparent communication improves trust
Audit trail of every action is important

## O - Objects

Tenant records
Room records and occupancy map
Fee ledger, receipts, and due alerts
Complaint tickets and resolution logs
Role-based dashboards and reports

### Inventory notes

Master data: rooms, beds, pricing, policies
Transactional data: admissions, payments, complaints
Evidence data: images/documents for complaints and identity

## U - Users

Tenant / Student
Admin / Hostel manager
Warden / Maintenance staff
Parent / Guardian (limited visibility)

### Roles in context

Tenant: self-service actions and tracking
Admin: control and approvals
Staff: operational execution

### User scene summary

Needs are speed, clarity, safety, and accountability
Product should support both daily operations and peak periods


# Sheet 2: Empathy Mapping

## Primary User

Tenant / Student living in hostel or PG

## Stakeholders

- Hostel owner / admin
- Warden and maintenance staff
- Parents/guardians
- Accounting/support team

## User Activities

- Register and upload details
- Check available rooms and confirm booking
- Pay fees and download receipts
- Raise complaints and track status
- Receive notices and reminders
- Install PWA on phone for quick daily access

## Storyboard (Short Flow)

1. User searches for room and registers.
2. Admin verifies details and allocates room.
3. User pays fee and gets confirmation.
4. User raises maintenance complaint when needed.
5. Staff resolves issue; user receives update.

## What User Says

- "I want quick room booking."
- "I need clear fee details and due dates."
- "I should not chase admin for complaint updates."
- "I want an app on my phone without downloading from a store."

## What User Thinks

- "Is my payment safely recorded?"
- "Will my issue be solved on time?"
- "Can I trust room and facility information?"

## What User Does

- Uses phone first for most actions
- Checks reminders near due dates
- Contacts admin if status is unclear

## What User Feels

### Happy Moments

- Fast registration and room confirmation
- Simple payment process with instant receipt
- Timely complaint closure
- One-tap open from home screen (installed PWA)

### Sad / Pain Moments

- Delay in room allocation
- Unclear dues or duplicate payment confusion
- No response on complaints

## Empathy Insights

- Users value speed, transparency, and safety
- Trust increases when updates are real-time
- Simple UI and status tracking matter most

City hostels and PGs with medium to high occupancy

Build a reliable digital system for hostel/PG operations
Solve manual errors, communication delays, and unclear records
Serve a real need with scalable workflows
Position as a product that can be sold / licensed to multiple hostels

User should feel clarity, control, and trust

Tenant / student (primary user)
Admin / hostel manager (primary operator)

Retain: fast flows, clear statuses, trusted reports, installable PWA
Repeat improvement after each feedback cycle


# Sheet 5: Scale Strategy (One Hostel Now, Many Later)

## Decision

Ship a solid product for ONE hostel first.
Design data and code as if MANY hostels will share the same system later.
Do NOT build billing, signup-of-hostels, or super-admin SaaS UI in v1.

## Rule

Every business row belongs to a hostel.
Always filter by hostel_id in APIs and queries.
v1: seed exactly one hostel; all users/rooms/fees/complaints attach to it.
v2: add more hostel rows — same code, no rewrite.

## Core tables (hostel-scoped)

hostels (id, name, address, settings…)
users (…, hostel_id, role)
rooms (…, hostel_id)
allocations (…, hostel_id)
fees / payments (…, hostel_id)
complaints (…, hostel_id)
notices (…, hostel_id)

JWT carries: user_id, role, hostel_id
Never trust hostel_id from the client body alone — take it from the logged-in user.

## What v1 includes

One hostel record in seed data
Full tenant / admin / staff flows for that hostel
hostel_id on all scoped tables and in auth context

## What waits until someone pays (v2+)

Create/manage multiple hostels
Cross-hostel super-admin
Per-hostel branding / custom domain
Subscriptions and invoices between you and owners

## Why this works

One hostel demo is complete and sellable as a pilot.
Adding hostel #2 becomes data + auth scope, not a new project.
